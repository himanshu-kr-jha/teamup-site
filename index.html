Why TeamUp

Five apps, five bills, five places your data lives.

A message in one app, the call in another, notes in a third, tasks and the calendar somewhere else. Each one charges per person and keeps a copy of your work. TeamUp puts the whole day in one place, on a server you own.

Chat appper seat Video appper seat Note-takerper seat Task trackerper seat Calendarper seat TeamUpone server

  • One flow, not five tabs

    A message becomes a call, the call becomes minutes, the minutes become tasks with owners. Nobody copies anything between apps.

  • Your server, your data

    Messages, files, calls and tasks live on a machine you control, in Postgres and S3 storage you can back up and inspect.

  • Pay for a server, not for seats

    The software is free. The 51st person you add costs the same as the 50th: nothing more.

Product tour

A working day in TeamUp

Real screens, recorded in the app, at the made-up company Nimbus Labs. Scroll and the screen follows.

teamup.nimbuslabs.test/chat

  1. Chat that keeps the thread

    Channels, direct messages, threads, reactions and read receipts. @mentions notify the person, voice and video messages record on the spot, and PDFs open right in the conversation.

  2. One tap, the channel is on a call

    Voice and video calls from any conversation, for many people at once, with screen sharing and a screen to check your camera and microphone before you join.

  3. Minutes write themselves

    A visible note-taker joins while an administrator has it on. After the call, everybody who was there gets the summary, the decisions and each action item with its owner.

  4. One board the whole team can see

    Tasks with assignees, labels, comments, watchers and due dates you can change or remove. Blocked tasks say what they are waiting for.

  5. Stand-ups for just your team

    Standing meetings for chosen teams or people, on the days and time you pick. Attendance comes from the call itself.

  6. Meetings and focus time, one calendar

    Month, week and day views, open slots and availability across people, Google Calendar sync, and calls shown for the time you were really in them.

  7. Find anything, on any screen

    Full-text and fuzzy search that only shows what you are allowed to see. On a phone, TeamUp installs to the home screen and every screen fits.

  8. See how hard your server works

    Processors, memory, disk, people in calls and the last backup, on one page for administrators, with a public status page for everybody else.

    Security and self-hosting

    Your server. Your data. You can see where it goes.

    TeamUp is one binary next to Postgres, Redis, S3 storage and LiveKit, all on a machine you run. Press a button to follow the data.

    Swipe sideways to see the whole server.

    A message goes from your browser to TeamUp, is saved in Postgres, and Redis hands it to every teammate's open connection. It never leaves your server.

    • Sign-in you control. Password login with signed tokens, refresh rotation that catches reused tokens, and a list of sessions you can revoke.
    • Custom roles and permissions. Fine-grained rules, checked on the server and the client. Search only shows what you may see.
    • Files never pass through the app. Browsers upload and download straight to your S3 storage with signed links, including resumable uploads for big files.
    • Run it like grown-up software. Structured logs, Prometheus metrics, an honest readiness check, a public status page, and nightly backups set up by the server script.
    • Outside services only when you switch them on. Your mail server, Google Calendar sync, and the note-taker's speech to text and minutes model. Turn the note-taker off and call audio stays home.

    Good to know, before you choose

    • Password sign-in is the supported way in. Google and Microsoft sign-in are not finished.
    • Recording needs LiveKit Egress added to the production stack. Calls work without it.
    • Keep /metrics behind the shipped nginx config, as it has no sign-in.
    • Not built yet: federation, end-to-end encryption, retention policies.

    FAQ

    Straight answers

    What does TeamUp cost?

    The software is free under the MIT licence. You pay for the server you run it on, and that cost does not grow with every person you add. There is no price per seat.

    What do I need to host it?

    One Linux VM with Docker Compose is the recommended path. It runs TeamUp with Postgres, Redis, MinIO and LiveKit, and a guide takes you from a fresh Ubuntu server to a running team. In our load test, a server with 2 processors carried about 500 busy people. Files can live in Amazon S3, Cloudflare R2 or Backblaze B2 instead.

    Where does our data live? Does anything leave the server?

    Messages, tasks, the calendar and files stay in your Postgres and your S3 storage, and call media goes through LiveKit on your server. Things leave only when you switch them on: your mail server for email, Google Calendar sync, and the meeting note-taker, which sends call audio to Sarvam for speech to text and the transcript to the minutes model you pick, or to your own model.

    Does it work on phones?

    Yes. Every screen fits phones and tablets, and TeamUp installs to an Android or iPhone home screen. There is also an Android app that opens the same web app of your server, with iPhone to follow.

    What is not there yet?

    Google and Microsoft sign-in are not finished, so password sign-in is the way in. Recording needs LiveKit Egress added in production. Federation, end-to-end encryption, native mobile apps and retention policies are not built. The README keeps this list honest.

    Is it ready for a real team?

    v1.0.0 is released, and Cognecto runs its working day on it: stand-ups every morning, dozens of people in calls, and the tasks and scrum that come out of them.

    Can people outside the company join a call?

    Yes. Guests join a meeting by its link with their name and a passcode, and the organizer can remove them.
