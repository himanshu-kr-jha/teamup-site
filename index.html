    05 Security and ownership

    Your server. Your data. You can see where it goes.

    TeamUp is one binary next to Postgres, Redis, S3 storage and LiveKit, all on a machine you run. Press a button to follow the data.

    Swipe sideways to see the whole server.

    A message goes from your browser to TeamUp, is saved in Postgres, and Redis hands it to every teammate's open connection. It never leaves your server.

    • Sign-in you control. Password login with signed tokens, refresh rotation that catches reused tokens, and a list of sessions you can revoke.
    • Custom roles and permissions. Fine-grained rules, checked on the server and the client. Search only shows what you may see.
    • Files never pass through the app. Browsers upload and download straight to your S3 storage with signed links, including resumable uploads for big files.
    • Run it like grown-up software. Structured logs, Prometheus metrics, an honest readiness check, a public status page, and nightly backups set up by the server script.
    • Outside services only when you switch them on. Your mail server, Google Calendar sync, and the note-taker's speech to text and minutes model. Turn the note-taker off and call audio stays home.

    How it runs on your server

    • One VM with Docker Compose. The recommended way: TeamUp with Postgres, Redis, file storage and LiveKit on one Linux server.
    • A single binary. One file with the web app inside it, and a systemd unit, is a real option too.
    • Kubernetes. A Helm chart, still beta: it renders and validates, but has not been installed on a live cluster yet.
    • Files in your own bucket. Amazon S3, Cloudflare R2 or Backblaze B2 work as well as the built-in storage.

    Good to know, before you choose

    • Password sign-in is the supported way in. Google and Microsoft sign-in are not finished.
    • Recording needs LiveKit Egress added to the production setup. Calls work without it.
    • The metrics page has no sign-in, so it stays behind the shipped nginx setup.
    • Not built yet: federation, end-to-end encryption, retention policies.

    FAQ

    Straight answers

    Is it a subscription?

    No. Your company buys a one-time licence and runs TeamUp on its own server. Updates and help with the server are separate, and only if you want them.

    Do we pay per user?

    No. The licence covers your company, so adding people does not add a monthly fee. Your server is the one running cost, and a bigger team may need a bigger server.

    What do updates cost?

    New versions are quoted per release or as a plan. You decide whether and when to update; the version you have keeps working.

    Can our own IT team run it?

    Yes. Your IT team can install and maintain TeamUp themselves, or we can set up and look after the server for you, priced separately.

    What do I need to host it?

    One Linux VM with Docker Compose is the recommended path. It runs TeamUp with Postgres, Redis, MinIO and LiveKit, and we can set it up for you, or your IT team can follow the guide from a fresh Ubuntu server to a running team. In our load test, a server with 2 processors carried about 500 busy people. Files can live in Amazon S3, Cloudflare R2 or Backblaze B2 instead.

    Where does our data live? Does anything leave the server?

    Messages, tasks, the calendar and files stay in your Postgres and your S3 storage, and call media goes through LiveKit on your server. Things leave only when you switch them on: your mail server for email, Google Calendar sync, and the meeting note-taker, which sends call audio to Sarvam for speech to text and the transcript to the minutes model you pick, or to your own model.

    Does it work on phones?

    Yes. Every screen fits phones and tablets, and TeamUp installs to an Android or iPhone home screen. There is also an Android app that opens the same web app of your server, with iPhone to follow.

    What is not there yet?

    Google and Microsoft sign-in are not finished, so password sign-in is the way in. Recording needs LiveKit Egress added in production. Federation, end-to-end encryption, native mobile apps and retention policies are not built.

    Is it ready for a real team?

    Yes. Cognecto runs its working day on TeamUp 1.0: stand-ups every morning, dozens of people in calls, and the tasks and scrum that come out of them.

    Can people outside the company join a call?

    Yes. Guests join a meeting by its link with their name and a passcode, and the organizer can remove them.

    We show TeamUp running a team's day, answer your questions about running it on your server, and look at whether it fits how your team works. If it does, we send a quote.
