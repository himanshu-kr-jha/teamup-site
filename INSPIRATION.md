# Inspiration for the second design

The second design borrows page patterns from SaaS landing pages as they looked before 2023.
Snapshots come from the Wayback Machine (`https://web.archive.org/web/<timestamp>/<site>`).
Only patterns are borrowed: no text, images, logos or code from these sites.

## Linear, October 2021 (snapshot 20211016130857)

A small release pill, a short headline and one line of subhead, then a single huge app screenshot centred directly under the hero, glowing against the dark page.
A logo row comes straight after, followed by a compact grid of short spec-like claims.
**Borrowed:** the centred hero with the product window right under it, the thin line of news above the header, and the row of facts under the window (load test, one-time licence, one binary, phones).

## Stripe, March 2021 (snapshot 20210301003103)

Every section repeats one unit: a small coloured eyebrow, a big headline, a short paragraph and a link, next to product UI drawn in HTML rather than flat images.
Late in the page a band of four large numbers carries the scale story.
**Borrowed:** the numbered chapter unit (eyebrow with a chapter number, headline, paragraph, then the live HTML demo), and the band of large performance numbers.

## Basecamp, January 2021 (snapshot 20210114174600)

The page reads like a letter: a narrow column of plain-spoken prose, and a "before / after" framing of the problem near the top.
**Borrowed:** the "Why TeamUp" section as a short letter with "Before TeamUp" and "With TeamUp" paragraphs, and the plain-print section on buying (how it runs, what is not built yet) set as reading text rather than cards.

## Slack, October 2019 (snapshot 20191015012618)

A tabbed capability switcher shows one product screen per tab (conversations, files, search, calls) instead of a long scroll of screenshots.
**Borrowed:** the hero's product window has tabs for Chat, Calls, Meeting notes, Tasks and Calendar, each with its own recording and a one-paragraph caption.
This replaces the first design's scroll-following tour.

## Raycast, June 2022 (snapshot 20220603183932)

A dark bento grid of feature tiles, each with a small real UI crop, plus a values block about data staying local.
**Borrowed:** "A working day in TeamUp" is a bento grid of recorded screens (stand-ups, search, phone, server health), with the stand-up recording as the large tile.

## Loom 2021 and Notion 2020 (from memory; the archive did not serve them)

Loom's three-step "how it works" strip and Notion's "replaces these tools" row.
**Borrowed:** "How you buy" as three large numbered steps beside the cost calculator, and the "what TeamUp replaces" row in the letter section.

## Also looked at

Superhuman (January 2020): one idea per screen, and a benefit headline with the mechanism right under it, used for the chapter headings.
Pitch (May 2021) and Figma (May 2020): a steady cadence of one idea per section with changing composition, which is why no two chapters here share a layout.
Both lean on customer quotes; this site has none to show, so it uses none.
